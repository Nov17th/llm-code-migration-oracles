\section{Conclusion and Data Availability}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We mapped how 49 papers at ICSE, FSE and ASE from 2024 to 2026 decide whether LLM-translated or migrated code behaves like the original. Compilation and existing tests dominate, and \new{legacy} settings fall back on differential runs, generated tests, LLM judges or people. \new{Of the 33 papers that report several oracles on the same outputs, 18 report both a compile and a behavioral rate, and there behavior trails compilation by up to 98 percentage points. The gaps to real migration are clear: legacy settings are rare, test evidence rests largely on benchmarks, derived oracles are seldom independent of the output, judgments by people or LLMs are rarely validated, compilation is reported as if it were evidence, and 9 papers state no limitation of their own oracle.} Before we trust LLMs to translate the software that runs the world, we must agree on how to check their work; the provenance checklist in Section~\ref{sec:checklist} is a first step.

We mapped how 49 papers at ICSE, FSE and ASE from 2024 to 2026 decide whether LLM-translated or migrated code behaves like the original. \new{Compilation (35 papers) and existing tests (28) dominate, while only 14 papers run the original program to compare behavior (RQ1). In translation between languages, existing tests come mostly from benchmarks; in library and version migration, they usually come from the project’s own suite. None of the 5 papers with a legacy source uses them, and 9 of the 12 papers on library migration include a human check (RQ2). Where papers report several oracles on the same outputs, the results often disagree. The compile rate exceeds the behavioral rate by more than 10 percentage points in 13 of the 18 papers that give both. In 4 of the 5 papers that place a similarity score next to a pass rate, the score reverses at least one ranking of systems or settings. Weak or few tests are the limitation that papers name most often, and 10 papers state none for their own oracle. Only 3 of the 17 papers with human evaluation report agreement between raters (RQ3).}

\new{The pilot shows the same effect when only the oracle varies. Across oracles and thresholds, the share of one fixed set of 164 translations counted as correct ranges from 52\% to 90\%.\cmtpink{P74, Major; EIC, R1, DA}{Same issue as in the abstract: give the main-threshold range (67\% to 90\%, 80\% under the tests) and name the setup (a 7B translator, one run).} At least one of the three oracles that do not execute the code disagrees with the tests on 46\% of the tasks. A small LLM judge accepts half of the translations that fail the tests and rejects 41\% of the benchmark's own reference solutions (RQ4). Reported success rates therefore depend on the oracle as well as on the system under evaluation.}

\new{Together, these findings form five gaps between current practice and what a migration needs (RQ5)\cmtpink{P75, Minor; EIC, DA}{Closer to the evidence than the abstract's needs of real migration projects; use one wording in the abstract, RQ5, Section 6 and here.}: compile rates and similarity scores diverge from behavioral results (G1); judgments by people or LLMs are rarely validated (G2); test evidence for translation rests largely on benchmarks (G3); some tests are not independent of the system under evaluation (G4); and most studies do not run the original program (G5). Each gap ends with a recommendation for studies that evaluate migration. The checklist for oracle provenance (Table~\ref{tab:checklist}) gathers these recommendations and two items outside the gaps for authors to follow and reviewers to check. Questions that better reporting alone cannot answer, such as which oracles catch which faults, are taken up in the directions for further study (Section~\ref{sec:rq5}).}
